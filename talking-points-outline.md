## Honest Conversation: Fear vs. Criticism

- Two concepts I want to cover that can help you manage AI change in your organization.
- 
Fear is personal, and comes from not knowing.
- Most fears can be addressed with clear communication about the decisions and direction leaders set. Fear is within your control.
- Criticism is usually a macro-level factor, out of your control.
- You don't get to solve criticism. You acknowledge it, weigh it, and decide if AI is still worth it.
- Both fear and criticism are valid.
- We'll touch on a few of the big ones. This is not an exhaustive list. We could be here for weeks. Contact me offline if you'd like more.

## Honest Conversation: Fear, job loss. AI supporting your people.

- On the slide: Klarna cut people for AI, then its CEO admitted they went too far and rehired.
- This is probably the biggest fear, and it is justified. Some companies are cutting back or freezing hiring.
- I don't agree with that approach. Freed-up time can go to value-add work and new initiatives that were not possible before AI.
- Honestly, I have never had so much work, in a good way. I can start and finish projects I could not even have started before.
- Thin margins and lack of resources are real, especially for small non-profits. But that is a resource issue. Don't blame it on AI.
- Hiring and firing decisions are leadership decisions.
- If you are bringing in AI to enhance your organization with the people you have, say so directly. That addresses the fear.
- The broader pattern for small organizations is growth, not cuts.
- 82 percent of small businesses using AI grew their workforce over the past year.
- 87 percent of small-business owners say AI augments their staff, not replaces them.
- Sources: U.S. Chamber of Commerce, Empowering Small Business 2025, and Goldman Sachs 10,000 Small Businesses Program.
- You choose whether the strategy is to increase throughput, or to increase at the margins.
- Transition: now moving to criticisms. Some are macro level and largely out of an organization's control.

## Honest Conversation: Criticism, environmental cost. The drain on resources is real.

- Delivery note: say this flat, not defensively.
- AI has a real environmental cost in energy and resources. Your organization's choices don't erase it.
- If you decide not to use AI, there will be nine more who do.
- Your decision won't stop the hundreds of billions going into the data centers that power AI.
- You can manage your footprint: how much AI you adopt, and how you use other resources.
- Ultimately, weigh it. Is your footprint justifiable for the benefit your organization produces?
- Even an environmental non-profit: analyze your footprint and outcomes, weigh them, decide, and accept the implications.
- When someone calls you out, you have the data and the reasoning. It won't make everyone happy, but it shows you take it seriously.

## Meet Allen: Set him up for success.

- No writing here. The writing comes on the next slide, after the example.
- You've selected the task. A good manager makes sure he has the tools and background to succeed.
- Would you hand him a task with no context or clear direction? No.
- On day one, explain the background, the clear goal, and the output you want. The clearer you are, the better Allen performs.
- Background: related templates, background documents, access to the right spreadsheets or data sets.
- Guardrails: the do's and don'ts. 'Don't send anything until my final approval.' 'Ensure no identifying data is included in the report.'
- Output and format: bullet points; formal and professional; a table with these columns and these fields.

## Meet Allen: Allen says he's done. How do you check? (Writing pause, 3 minutes)

- Pause: writing exercise, 3 minutes. Circulate.
- As the manager of this new intern on day one, you should rightfully be a little skeptical.
- How would you check his work? Any vulnerabilities you'd double-check? What is your method for reviewing?
- This is the most critical step when using AI. It is ultimately your responsibility and your credibility.
- It is also your chance to see what feedback to give Allen, so he does better on the next try.
- My example: count the survey forms for each event, and make sure that many were recorded.
- Spot check a couple of the rating averages. And read through the whole summary.
- What's the point of a summary if you don't read it? Some folks ask AI for a summary of a summary, and lose real information.
- Make sure there is still substance. AI can be really good at writing fluff: saying a lot but not much at all. Sort of like me.
